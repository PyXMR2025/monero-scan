# Would a small shared decision checklist be useful?
author: SlowBearDigger
created_at: 2026-07-11T21:26:38Z
status: closed

I have been thinking about something after the recent Community Workgroup meeting, and I wanted to put it here before going too far with it.

Sometimes a group around Monero needs to make a decision that affects shared resources, access, funds, an event, or work that somebody will have to carry out later. Most of the time open discussion and rough agreement works fine. I don't think that should change.

But there are also cases where the discussion ends and people are still not completely sure what was decided, who the decision applied to, or who is supposed to do something after it.

To be clear, I am not talking about one process for Monero. MoneroKon is not the CCS, the CCS is not the Community Workgroup, Core is another workgroup with its own responsibilities, and independent projects remain independent. None of them should be able to use a document like this to speak for the others.

I originally started thinking about a voting tool. While working on it I realised voting is probably the last part, not the first. A perfect count does not help much if the question, participants, available options or practical outcome were never clear.

## Some history behind the idea

I tried to look for similar discussions before posting this.

The old FFS process was deliberately loose, but contributors still decided to [write down the process, caveats and contingencies](https://github.com/monero-project/meta/issues/63). That did not create a central structure. It made an existing process easier to understand.

There was also a long discussion about [decentralizing community development](https://github.com/monero-project/meta/issues/237). One of the important points there was that workgroups should be able to form by themselves, use their own resources and create alternatives. So anything proposed here would need to be optional and easy to fork, otherwise it would be going in the wrong direction.

The [2018 Core Team announcement](https://www.getmonero.org/2018/03/01/core-team-announcement.html) makes a useful distinction too. Core looks after some infrastructure and resources where somebody has to, but Core does not equal Monero and nobody needs Core's permission to start doing something.

The [CCS escrow discussion in 2023](https://github.com/monero-project/meta/issues/935) is another interesting example. There was a Yes, No, Maybe poll, but the hard questions were not really about counting those answers. People disagreed about what the escrow role included, duplicate accounts, alternatives to a single custodian and whether anyone was actually ready to operate those alternatives.

There are also good examples of handovers and emergency responses. The [technical responsibilities update from 2019](https://www.getmonero.org/2019/12/16/technical-responsibilities-update.html) listed responsibilities and backups in public. After the compromised binaries incident, the [community post mortem meeting](https://www.getmonero.org/2019/11/23/community-meeting.html) discussed verification, communication, responsible people and alternates.

So Monero does have safeguards already. They are just different depending on the group and the resources involved. Maybe the useful thing is not to invent a new system, but to collect a few of the practices that have worked.

## What I have in mind

Something short, probably one page. A group could use it when it feels a decision needs a clearer record.

It could answer things like:

* What exactly are we deciding, and what is outside the question?
* Which group and resources are actually involved?
* How can somebody take part in this particular discussion?
* What options and serious objections are still open?
* How will the group tell that it has reached an outcome?
* Who volunteered to carry it out, and what happens if they cannot?
* If credentials or shared resources are involved, is there a backup and a handover path?
* Where will the final record be kept?

This would only describe one decision in one scope. A Reddit poll, GitHub reactions, meeting attendance or use of the Monero name would not make it apply to everybody else.

It also would not stop another group from doing things differently. People could copy the note, change it, ignore it or replace it.

## Critical situations

There is one awkward part that I don't think should be ignored. Sometimes credentials are compromised, a custodian disappears, infrastructure is actively failing, or waiting could cause an irreversible loss.

In that case the people who already look after the affected resource may need to act before a normal discussion can finish. This would not create a new role or new power. It would only record:

* what was at risk
* why waiting was unsafe
* what limited action was taken
* who else can verify or reverse it
* when the action expires
* when an explanation and review will be published

The point should be to protect options, not to choose winners or settle an ordinary disagreement. No permanent change should be smuggled through as an emergency action.

## What I am not proposing

I am not trying to define who belongs to Monero, create one voter list, rank contributors, use XMR balances as voting power, or put different workgroups under the same structure.

I am also not suggesting that old decisions should be reopened. Past cases are useful to test whether the checklist explains things clearly, not to relitigate them.

And I would not have any special role because I opened the issue or wrote a prototype.

## Maybe a first test

Before building more software or asking anyone to adopt something, we could take a few old and non-sensitive examples from clearly separate groups and try the checklist on them.

If it makes the scope, outcome and practical next steps clearer, then we simplify it and publish a one page draft. If it feels controlling or just adds ceremony, we drop it. Simple as that.

Only after a group says it actually needs a formal poll would it make sense to discuss a verifiable voting extension. I have some prototype work around that, but I don't think it should lead this discussion.

I would really appreciate corrections from people who have worked on FFS/CCS, infrastructure, independent workgroups, events or past community meetings.

Is a small optional checklist like this useful at all? What existing Monero practices am I missing, and what parts of this could accidentally imply control over people or projects that never opted in?

I can do the initial research and drafting, but I don't want to own the result. If something useful comes out of it, it should be written in public and stay easy for anybody to change or fork.
